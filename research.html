Understanding the aging brain and mind
As people age, some cognitive abilities decline while emotional and social functioning can remain surprisingly stable, or even improve. My research asks why: what changes in the brain drive age-related shifts in "cold" cognition, such as memory and executive function, versus "hot" cognition, such as socioemotional behavior and decision-making? I study these questions across the spectrum from normal aging to Alzheimer's disease (AD), with the broader goal of identifying factors that support successful cognitive aging.

A multimodal, data-driven approach
I combine multimodal neuroimaging (fMRI, structural MRI, and PET), machine learning, and longitudinal statistical modeling to link brain structure and function to behavior over time. I work with both lab-collected data and large open-access and clinical-trial datasets, which lets me test whether findings generalize across cohorts that differ in demographics, recruitment, and measurement protocols, an important step toward research that translates into real-world clinical benefit.

Current project: U.S. POINTER
I am currently a Postdoctoral Scholar in Dr. Susan Landau's lab at the Helen Wills Neuroscience Institute, University of California, Berkeley, where I work on the U.S. Study to Protect Brain Health Through Lifestyle Intervention to Reduce Risk (U.S. POINTER). I lead analyses of behavioral, structural MRI, and PET imaging data to investigate lifestyle and biomarker predictors of cognitive aging, and contribute to statistical modeling and manuscript development for the study.

Earlier work
During my Ph.D. at the University of Florida, my research examined how intranasal oxytocin modulates large-scale brain networks (including the salience network and amygdala) in older adults, and how brain structure and connectivity relate to individual differences in procrastination, self-control, and delay discounting. This earlier work shaped my current interest in how neuromodulatory and network-level brain changes relate to cognition and behavior across the lifespan.
